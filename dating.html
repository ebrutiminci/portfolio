Dating App ResearchTesting the problem before the idea

The idea on the table was a dating app with AI built in, to help people past the first messages. Before anyone builds it, I tested the problem instead: a 4-minute survey that never mentions AI, with rules written in advance for what would prove the idea wrong.

"What would prove me wrong?" Written down before the first answer came in

Plan

From an idea to a decision

I ran the study through the loop my UX Research App is built on. The founder's answers become bets, the riskiest bet becomes H1, and every survey question and every result points back to a hypothesis. Here, H1 was: people give up at the first messages.

Before sharing the survey, I wrote three rules that would tell me I was wrong. Writing them first means I can't move the goalposts once the answers arrive.

Rule, written beforeWhat came backVerdict

Research

A survey that never names the idea

If you ask "would you use an AI that writes your first message?", people say yes to be nice. So the survey only asks about the past: what frustrated you last time, where you usually give up, what you did about it. Its 14 questions fall into six groups: ask or not, who, what they use, pains and gains, how they see it, and contact.

  1. 01 · ScreenerAsk or notNon-users answer two "why not" questions and stop, so every number is about real users
  2. 02 · ScriptBuildThe Google Form is generated by an Apps Script, with branching and shuffled options
  3. 03 · RulesCommitThe pass/fail rules are written down before the link goes out
  4. 04 · ShareCollectr/SampleSize, r/takemysurvey and friends, 29 Oct to 10 Nov 2026

Synthesize

Eleven people, every answer

With 11 people, a percentage hides more than it shows, so every chart counts people. One page holds the whole survey: each spoke is a person, each ring an answer, the lines in the centre join people who share a frustration, and the strands on the right follow each person from what they want to where they quit.

The groups come from one question only, "What are you mainly looking for?", plus gender and age. My first version also grouped people by their answers, and then "found" that those groups answered differently. That is circular, so I rebuilt the groups and every chart.

General notes

How I made it

I counted every answer with Python straight from the form's export, then designed and coded the charts in d3 with Claude Code and turned them into a findings deck. The founder gets one folder: a two-page "start here" PDF, an 11-slide deck, a spreadsheet with every answer and the cross-tabs, and each slide as an image.

Then I wrote the whole method down as rules: count people, never percentages; group by one question only; check the pass/fail rules first; sort every bet into held, didn't hold, or surprise. Those rules are now the spec for the next step of my research app, so any founder can add their survey and get the same folder back.

Privacy: people appear only as P1 to P14. Emails were replaced with "left an email: yes or no", and one offensive answer was removed. With 14 people, every number here is a signal, not proof.

The mistake I now check every time

Group people by the answers you then present as findings.

Circular · makes fake correlations

Make the groups from one question only, then compare them on everything else.
